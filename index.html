Apr–Dec 2025

Complex admittance profiling of animal tissues to study postmortem interval

Undergraduate honours thesis, University of Peradeniya. Supervisor: Dr. C. P. Jayalath

I built an open-ended coaxial probe from a lathed UHF connector and used a calibrated nanoVNA-F V2 to track the complex admittance of chicken skin over the first 12 hours after death, at 101 frequencies from 50 kHz to 100 MHz. I wrote a Python viewer that converts the reflection data to admittance and plots Nyquist and single-frequency curves.

The raw data were noisy, mostly from probe contact. 7th-order fits still showed coherent trends. The real part rose and then levelled off or declined. At low frequencies the imaginary part was larger, and at high frequencies the real part was larger. Next steps are a spring-loaded probe holder, a better analyzer and ethical clearance for more samples.

Theoretical physics

Preprint 2026

A Toy Model of Time Dilation as a Superposition of Time-Rate Branches on a Finite Rapidity Lattice

Consistency conditions and experimental bounds

A toy model that treats time dilation as a superposition of time-rate branches on a finite rapidity lattice, with consistency conditions and bounds from experiment.

doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23031106

Try the idea: a boost slides the weight along a lattice of rapidities

Each rung of the lattice is a branch with its own time rate. Sharp at one rung, the clock slows by the usual 1/γ. Spread over several, the clock rate is the weighted average of its branches.

A sketch from the model’s basic ingredients, not the paper’s full construction. Rapidity spacing is 0.1 per rung. The preprint has the consistency conditions and experimental bounds.

In short. Time dilation is modelled as several time-rate branches in superposition, on a finite lattice of rapidities, and then checked against consistency conditions and experimental bounds.

2025

ER = EPR and the holographic principle

Undergraduate seminar poster and presentation

I examined how geometric structures such as Einstein–Rosen bridges may emerge from quantum entanglement, and what that means for the microstructure of spacetime.

Try the idea: turn up the entanglement and watch a bridge open

Two particles, A and B, share no link at first. As their entanglement grows, the ER = EPR conjecture says the geometry between them grows a bridge too. Entanglement (EPR) and wormhole (Einstein–Rosen) are two descriptions of one connection.

A cartoon of the conjecture, not a calculation. The slider is a stand-in for how entangled the pair is, from none to maximal.

Read my poster (PDF)

The idea. A wormhole-like bridge in spacetime may be what quantum entanglement looks like in geometry.
